\begin{abstract}
We give a computer-assisted proof of positive definiteness for a frozen
corrected gauged-linear-sigma-model (GLSM) quotient metric along a connected
one-parameter path on an explicit compact toric Calabi--Yau threefold with
$(h^{2,1},h^{1,1})=(5,81)$.  A parameterized Krawczyk construction proves
existence and local uniqueness of the implicit hypersurface--moment-map branch
for $d\in[1.49985,1.5]$.  The branch is covered by seven root patches and seven
uniqueness bridges, with every componentwise Krawczyk ratio below $0.85$.
Positive definiteness of the corrected $3\times3$ Hermitian pullback metric is
then established on 1003 exactly adjacent interval segments by rational lower
bounds for its three leading principal minors.  A separate formula
implementation reconstructs the weakest third-minor segment in each of six
metric patches, and an independent 83-real endpoint construction recomputes
the corrected determinants using exact rational interval arithmetic.  At the
endpoint a fixed direction ensemble also proves a relative correction larger
than $1.83\%$.  The result is a theorem about a specified frozen $\ddbar$
ansatz on a one-dimensional path.  It is not a global Ricci-flat metric, a
derivation of the physical $\alpha'$ correction, or a complete string vacuum.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Yau's theorem guarantees a Ricci-flat K\"ahler metric in each K\"ahler class
of a compact Calabi--Yau manifold, but explicit metrics on compact
threefolds are generally unavailable.  Numerical work based on balanced
metrics and energy functionals made these metrics computationally accessible
\cite{Donaldson2005,Douglas2008,Braun2008,Headrick2013}.  Machine-learning
methods have subsequently enlarged the range of geometries and moduli that
can be studied \cite{Ashmore2020,Anderson2021,Larfors2022}.  These methods
measure approximation quality by sampled or integrated residuals; they do
not ordinarily produce mathematically rigorous enclosures at every point of
a parameter interval.

Corrections to an internal Calabi--Yau metric are directly relevant to string
compactifications.  In particular, Ref.~\cite{FraserTaliente2024} computes a
leading $\alpha'^3$ correction to a numerically approximated quintic metric.
That work is the closest physical antecedent to the present calculation, but
its neural approximation and percent-level residual are not interval
certificates.  Rigorous interval methods have entered nearby Calabi--Yau
problems, for example the computer-assisted monodromy calculation for a toric
K3 family in Ref.~\cite{Ishige2025}, but we found no previous interval proof
of corrected-metric positivity on an explicit compact Calabi--Yau
continuation branch.  To the best of our knowledge, the result below is the
first computer-assisted interval-arithmetic positivity theorem for a
nontrivially corrected GLSM quotient metric along a connected continuation
path on an explicit compact Calabi--Yau threefold used in a
string-compactification model.  This priority statement concerns the
combination, not any individual GLSM, interval, or Krawczyk ingredient.

The threefold is the $(5,81)$ example occurring in the explicit type-IIB
compactifications of Ref.~\cite{Demirtas2021}.  The role of this provenance is
to make the geometry non-toy and fully identifiable.  It does not imply that
the local metric theorem below certifies the full vacuum constructed in that
reference.

The central distinction of this paper is between discovery and proof.  The
correction coefficients were selected by a finite-sample approximation
experiment and then frozen as exact binary rationals.  Their origin does not
prove that the resulting tensor is positive.  Positivity follows only from a
subsequent interval calculation on a Krawczyk-certified implicit branch.

\section{Geometry and frozen metric ansatz}

Let $Q\in\mathbb Z^{81\times85}$ be the disclosed GLSM charge matrix and let
$r\in\mathbb R^{81}$ be the FI vector.  In a regular toric chart, write $z_i$
for the Cox coordinates and solve the moment-map equations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment}
 QD=r,\qquad
 D_i=|z_i|^2\exp((Q^Tu)_i),\qquad i=1,\ldots,85,
\end{equation}
for $u\in\mathbb R^{81}$.  With $D=\operatorname{diag}(D_i)$, the horizontal
ambient quotient tensor is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient}
 H=D-DQ^T(QDQ^T)^{-1}QD.
\end{equation}
The identities $Q[:,\text{divisor basis}]=I_{81}$ and $Q\,v_i=0$ fix the
linear identification with the toric K\"ahler data.  If $A$ is the analytic
tangent matrix obtained from the implicit hypersurface equation, the
baseline pullback metric can equivalently be evaluated as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pullback}
 \Gzero=A^*DA-(QDA)^*(QDQ^T)^{-1}(QDA).
\end{equation}

The correction is defined by a scalar function on the quotient.  Let
\begin{equation}
 S=\sum_{i=1}^{85}D_i,\qquad p_i=D_i/S,
\end{equation}
and let $S_0,p_i^{(0)}$ denote their values at the disclosed reference point.
For the archived rational direction $v\in\QQ^{85}$ define
\begin{equation}
 q=\frac{S-S_0}{S+S_0},\qquad
 s=\sum_{i=1}^{85}v_i\bigl(p_i-p_i^{(0)}\bigr)^2.
\end{equation}
The frozen correction potential is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:correction}
 \phi=c_1(\lambda_1q^2)^2+c_2(\lambda_2s)^2,
 \qquad
 \Gc=\Gzero+\ddbar\phi,
\end{equation}
where $c_1,c_2,\lambda_1,\lambda_2$, $v$, $S_0$, and all $p_i^{(0)}$ are
serialized as reduced rational pairs.  In particular,
\begin{equation}
 c_1=-\frac{3704684168964555}{9007199254740992},\qquad
 c_2=\frac{4163219474253507}{1125899906842624}.
\end{equation}
Because Eq.~\eqref{eq:correction} is a $\ddbar$ deformation of a scalar that
is invariant under the complexified GLSM action, it preserves the local
K\"ahler structure wherever the implicit quotient construction is regular.
The theorem below addresses positive definiteness, not the size of a global
Ricci or Monge--Amp\`ere residual.

\section{Certified implicit branch}

The parameter $d$ describes the disclosed affine continuation, and we set
$t=d-3/2$.  At each parameter patch the coupled implicit system consists of
the real and imaginary hypersurface equations and the 81 moment-map
equations, hence 83 real unknowns.  Denote this system by
\begin{equation}
 F(t,x)=0,\qquad x\in\mathbb R^{83}.
\end{equation}

For a parameter interval $T$, center $x_0$, box
$X=x_0+[-r_1,r_1]\times\cdots\times[-r_{83},r_{83}]$, and a midpoint inverse
$C$, the parameterized Krawczyk image is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:krawczyk}
 K(T,X)=x_0-CF(T,x_0)+\bigl(I-CF_x(T,X)\bigr)(X-x_0).
\end{equation}
Strict componentwise inclusion $K(T,X)\subset\operatorname{int}X$ proves
existence and uniqueness in the declared box for each $t\in T$; overlap
bridges identify neighboring local branches \cite{Moore2009,Breiding2023}.

The archived chain uses seven patches and seven bridges to cover
\begin{equation}
 t\in[-3/20000,0].
\end{equation}
The maximum exact-rational replayed image-to-radius ratios are
\begin{equation}
 q_{\rm patch}<0.849476916,
 \qquad q_{\rm bridge}<0.829319820,
\end{equation}
and both are strictly below the preregistered threshold $17/20$.  Every patch
and bridge contains 83 componentwise strict inequalities.  Overlap and an
independent endpoint enclosure identify one base-anchored branch throughout
the interval.

\begin{proposition}[Certified continuation branch]\label{prop:branch}
For every $d\in[1.49985,1.5]$ the disclosed 83-real implicit system has a
solution in the corresponding archived Krawczyk box.  The solution is unique
inside that box, and the overlap certificates identify these local solutions
as one connected branch.
\end{proposition}

The proposition does not assert that no other branch exists outside the
archived boxes.

\section{Metric interval theorem}

On each subinterval, interval implicit differentiation of the hypersurface
and moment-map systems gives all six real first and second derivatives needed
for the $3\times3$ Hermitian pullback tensor.  The inverse of $QDQ^T$ and the
joint implicit Jacobian is enclosed through a scaled finite Neumann series
with a rigorous geometric tail.  The metric is symmetrized by intersection
with its Hermitian transpose, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:correction} is differentiated
analytically with second-order interval jets.

Let $\Delta_k(d)$ be the determinant of the leading $k\times k$ principal
submatrix of $\Gc(d)$.  By the Hermitian Sylvester criterion,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sylvester}
 \Gc(d)\succ0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Delta_1(d)>0,\ \Delta_2(d)>0,\ \Delta_3(d)>0.
\end{equation}
The metric layer consists of six patches and 1003 exactly adjacent segments.

\begin{theorem}[Corrected-metric positivity on the continuation path]
\label{thm:metric}
Along the base-anchored branch of Proposition~\ref{prop:branch}, the frozen
corrected pullback metric of Eq.~\eqref{eq:correction} is positive definite
for every
\begin{equation}
 d\in[1.49985,1.5].
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The six metric covers contain 1003 nondegenerate rational intervals.  Exact
endpoint comparison shows adjacency within each patch, overlap between
patches, and union $[29997/20000,3/2]$.  On every segment, interval evaluation
gives strict positive rational lower bounds for $\Delta_1$, $\Delta_2$, and
$\Delta_3$.  Their minima are the positive fractions in
Table~\ref{tab:central}.  Proposition~\ref{prop:branch} identifies the
implicit solution boxes with one branch.  Equation~\eqref{eq:sylvester} then
proves positive definiteness at every parameter value on that branch.
\end{proof}

\section{Independent checks and correction materiality}

Serialized replay can detect gaps, sign failures, and stale attachments, but
it does not by itself detect a common formula error in the generator.  We
therefore add two checks with deliberately different boundaries.

First, a separate Arb implementation imports neither the primary metric
generator nor its adapter.  Before reconstruction, the weakest serialized
$\Delta_3$ segment is selected in each of the six metric patches.  The second
implementation rebuilds Eq.~\eqref{eq:pullback}, the correction jets, and all
three principal minors on these six segments.  All 18 independent lower
bounds are positive and contain the corresponding primary lower bounds.
These checks reuse the primary certified root boxes and are therefore a
formula replication, not a second proof of the entire continuation chain.

Second, at $d=1.5$ a separate 83-real Krawczyk box is constructed.  Its 83
strict inclusions prove a locally unique root, and the continuation endpoint
box lies componentwise inside this uniqueness box with maximum radius ratio
$10^{-4}$.  Independent baseline and correction implementations serialize
entrywise directed rational bounds.  A stdlib-only checker then adds the
complex interval matrices and recomputes all three determinants by the
Leibniz formula using exact \texttt{fractions.Fraction} arithmetic.  The
resulting lower bounds are positive.

To exclude a merely formal correction whose certified effect is
indistinguishable from zero, a fixed ensemble of 31 Gaussian-integer
directions is evaluated at the endpoint.  For two directions the rigorous
lower bound on
\begin{equation}
 \frac{|v^*(\Gc-\Gzero)v|}{v^*\Gzero v}
\end{equation}
exceeds one percent.  The largest certified lower bound is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{7568816278373741}{413272176438128128}
 =0.0183143620\ldots.
\end{equation}
This is an existence statement within a fixed finite direction ensemble, not
an operator-norm estimate along the full path.

\section{Reproducibility and proof architecture}

The release separates the heavy generator layer from the portable review
layer.  The heavy calculations use Arb midpoint-radius arithmetic
\cite{Johansson2017}, analytic implicit derivatives, and exact rational
serialization.  The public archive contains the resulting directed bounds,
the six metric covers, root bridges, endpoint witnesses, independent formula
checks, and their complete transitive SHA-256 provenance graph.  The graph
contains 88 visited nodes and 226 resolved edges, with no unresolved or
ambiguous proof edge.  Three failed diagnostics are retained in a historical
manifest but excluded from proof traversal.

The command
\begin{verbatim}
python verify_release.py
\end{verbatim}
requires only Python 3.11 or later.  It verifies the release manifest, checks
the exact rational continuation and positivity inequalities, independently
recomputes the endpoint determinants and materiality ratios, and runs
mutation tests.  The mutations delete a metric segment, replace a positive
minor by zero, and force equality in a bridge inclusion; every mutation must
be rejected.

The portable checker is intentionally not described as formal verification.
It trusts the correctness of Python integer arithmetic, SHA-256, the
serialized Arb enclosures, and the stated analytic formulas.  Conversely,
the heavy generator is not needed for last-mile replay and cannot silently
change after publication because every included byte is bound by the release
manifest.

\section{Scope, interpretation, and limitations}

The strongest admitted conclusion is Theorem~\ref{thm:metric}.  Several
nearby statements are not consequences of it.

First, the parameter interval is one-dimensional and has length
$1.5\times10^{-4}$.  Each metric evaluation reconstructs all transverse
derivatives needed for a three-complex-dimensional pullback metric, but this
does not prove positivity on an open six-real-dimensional tube.  Nor does it
cover the entire compact hypersurface or all toric charts.

Second, $\Gzero$ is a section-free GLSM quotient reference metric, not a
certified Ricci-flat Calabi--Yau metric.  The correction of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:correction} is a frozen gauge-invariant approximation ansatz.
The theorem proves that this specified correction preserves positive
definiteness on the declared path.  It does not derive the ansatz from the
ten-dimensional effective action, bound omitted $\alpha'$ terms, or certify
a global Ricci/Monge--Amp\`ere error.

Third, the phrase ``the $(5,81)$ model'' identifies an explicit geometry from
a realistic compactification program.  The result does not certify mobile
D3 fields, Pfaffians, uplift, a 113-field mass matrix, or phenomenology.
Accordingly, neither ``full physical vacuum'' nor ``global corrected
Calabi--Yau metric'' is an admissible description of this theorem.

\section{Conclusion}

We have exhibited a reviewer-replayable computer-assisted theorem in a regime
where numerical Calabi--Yau work has usually relied on sampled accuracy.  A
Krawczyk-certified 83-real branch, a gap-free 1003-segment metric cover,
strict Sylvester bounds, stratified formula replication, and an independent
endpoint determinant calculation together prove that a nonzero frozen
correction preserves metric positivity throughout a connected path on the
explicit $(5,81)$ geometry.  The natural next mathematical extension is an
open tubular neighborhood and then multi-chart coverage.  Those extensions
are separate results and are not assumed here.
