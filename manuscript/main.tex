\begin{abstract}
We give a computer-assisted proof of positive definiteness for a frozen
corrected gauged-linear-sigma-model (GLSM) quotient metric on a six-real-
dimensional product neighborhood in one explicit toric chart of a compact
Calabi--Yau threefold with $(h^{2,1},h^{1,1})=(5,81)$.  This consolidates the
central-path calculation, its independent endpoint validation, and the full
product-tube extension into one theorem hierarchy.  The parameter domain is
\[
 \TT=[-3/20000,0]\times[-10^{-8},10^{-8}]^5.
\]
A parameterized interval Krawczyk calculation certifies a locally unique
83-real-dimensional hypersurface--moment-map root on 1,084 overlapping parent
boxes.  A robust central continuation branch anchors these local roots, and
1,083 nonempty product overlaps glue them into one branch on $\TT$.  The
corrected $3\times3$ Hermitian pullback metric is reconstructed on 34,688
child boxes that exactly tile the transverse cube.  All 104,064 directed
rational intervals for its three leading principal minors have positive lower
bounds.  The smallest third-minor lower bound is
\[
 \frac{2100827914296867}{18889465931478580854784}>0.
\]
At the endpoint, a separate 83-real Krawczyk implementation and independent
baseline/correction matrix reconstruction reprove local positivity.  On a
fixed ensemble of 31 Gaussian-integer directions, two directions have a
certified relative correction above one percent; the largest lower bound is
$0.0183143620\ldots$.
The release separates a dependency-free exact last-mile verifier from a full
Arb formula-regeneration layer, validates integer geometry without rounding,
and includes fail-closed mutation tests.  The theorem is local to one chart
and one frozen K\"ahler ansatz; it is neither a global Calabi--Yau positivity
theorem nor a Ricci-flatness or physical-vacuum certificate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Yau's theorem gives a unique Ricci-flat K\"ahler metric in a fixed K\"ahler
class after normalization, but explicit compact Calabi--Yau metrics are rarely
available.  Numerical approaches based on balanced metrics, energy
functionals, and machine learning have made quantitative calculations
possible on increasingly complicated geometries
\cite{Donaldson2005,Douglas2008,Braun2008,Headrick2013,Ashmore2020,Anderson2021,Larfors2022}.
Their usual validation language is statistical or residual based.  It is
different from a uniform theorem in which every rounding and truncation error
is enclosed on a continuum of parameter values.

The present work develops the latter type of statement for an explicit
corrected GLSM quotient metric.  The geometry is the $(5,81)$ threefold used
in the type-IIB compactification data of Ref.~\cite{Demirtas2021}.  That
provenance makes the example identifiable and non-toy, but it does not transfer
the physical conclusions of that compactification to our local metric
calculation.  Related numerical work has studied leading metric corrections
on compact Calabi--Yau spaces \cite{FraserTaliente2024}; rigorous interval
methods have also appeared in neighboring Calabi--Yau computations, for
example in monodromy calculations \cite{Ishige2025}.  We make no bibliometric
priority claim here.  The mathematical contribution is the explicit theorem,
its complete box cover, and a release that permits exact replay of the final
inequalities.

The calculation was developed in two stages that are consolidated here.  The
first stage certified positivity along the central one-dimensional
continuation path and supplied an independently reconstructed endpoint root,
metric, and nonzero-correction witness.  A path theorem alone does not imply an
open-neighborhood theorem: transverse variables can alter both the implicit
root and the metric, and a finite collection of sampled corners is not a
cover.  The second stage proves the complete product tube.  Root existence and
uniqueness are established on parents of transverse radius $10^{-8}$; metric
positivity is then proved on their $2^5$ exact transverse children.  Earlier
versioned Zenodo/GitHub deposits are computational prereleases of this unified
manuscript, not separate peer-reviewed articles.

The distinction between discovery and proof is essential.  Correction
coefficients were obtained in an approximation stage and subsequently frozen
as exact binary rationals.  Their provenance does not prove positivity.
Positivity follows from interval inclusion for the implicit system and strict
rational lower bounds for the three principal minors.  Likewise, floating-
point contraction ratios retained for diagnostics are never proof inputs.

\section{Geometry and frozen corrected metric}

Let $Q\in\mathbb Z^{81\times85}$ denote the disclosed GLSM charge matrix.  In
the selected regular toric chart, let $z_i$ be Cox coordinates and solve the
moment-map equations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment}
 QD=r,\qquad D_i=|z_i|^2\exp((Q^Tu)_i),\qquad i=1,\ldots,85,
\end{equation}
for $u\in\mathbb R^{81}$.  Writing $D=\operatorname{diag}(D_i)$, the horizontal
ambient quotient tensor is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient}
 H=D-DQ^T(QDQ^T)^{-1}QD.
\end{equation}
If $A$ is the analytic tangent matrix obtained by implicit differentiation of
the hypersurface equation, the baseline pullback metric is evaluated in the
numerically more convenient form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pullback}
 \Gzero=A^*DA-(QDA)^*(QDQ^T)^{-1}(QDA).
\end{equation}

The correction is the complex Hessian of a frozen scalar.  Put
\begin{equation}
 S=\sum_{i=1}^{85}D_i,\qquad p_i=D_i/S,
\end{equation}
and write $S_0,p_i^{(0)}$ for their values at the disclosed reference point.
For an archived rational direction $v\in\QQ^{85}$, define
\begin{equation}
 q=\frac{S-S_0}{S+S_0},\qquad
 s=\sum_{i=1}^{85}v_i\bigl(p_i-p_i^{(0)}\bigr)^2.
\end{equation}
The potential and corrected metric are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:correction}
 \phi=c_1(\lambda_1q^2)^2+c_2(\lambda_2s)^2,
 \qquad \Gc=\Gzero+\ddbar\phi.
\end{equation}
All entries of $v$, all reference data, and $c_1,c_2,\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ are
stored as reduced rational pairs.  In particular,
\begin{equation}
 c_1=-\frac{3704684168964555}{9007199254740992},\qquad
 c_2=\frac{4163219474253507}{1125899906842624}.
\end{equation}
The scalar is invariant under the complexified GLSM action, so its
$\ddbar$ deformation is a well-defined local K\"ahler-form candidate on the
regular quotient chart.  The task below is to prove positive definiteness on
a declared domain, not to prove Ricci flatness.

The archived adapter historically represented the entries of $Q$ and the
hypersurface exponent vectors as JSON numbers, some spelled as integral
binary64 values such as $1.0$.  The publication implementation accepts such a
value only if it is exactly integral and has magnitude at most $2^{53}$.
There is no call to \texttt{round}.  The resulting $81\times85$ charge matrix,
ten $85$-component exponent vectors, coefficients, and chart indices are
serialized in a separate exact-integer registry and checked against the raw
inputs before verification.

\section{The product domain and implicit system}

Let $d$ be the affine continuation coordinate and set $t=d-3/2$.  Five real
transverse chart coordinates are denoted by $w=(w_1,\ldots,w_5)$.  The
certified parameter tube is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tube}
 \TT=\left[-\frac{3}{20000},0\right]
       \times\left[-\frac{1}{10^8},\frac{1}{10^8}\right]^5.
\end{equation}
The coupled real system consists of the real and imaginary hypersurface
equations and 81 moment-map equations:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F}
 F(t,w,x)=0,\qquad x\in\mathbb R^{83}.
\end{equation}

For a parameter box $P$, a center $x_0$, an unknown box $X=x_0+[-r,r]$
(with componentwise radii), and a midpoint inverse $C$, the parameterized
Krawczyk operator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:krawczyk}
 K(P,X)=x_0-CF(P,x_0)+\bigl(I-CF_x(P,X)\bigr)(X-x_0).
\end{equation}
Strict componentwise inclusion
$K(P,X)\subset\operatorname{int}X$ proves existence of a root for every
parameter in $P$ and uniqueness among roots in $X$; regularity follows from
the same enclosure \cite{Moore2009,Breiding2023}.  The complex hypersurface
elimination additionally stores a directed interval for its derivative and
checks that the complex rectangle excludes zero.

The longitudinal centers form an exact ordered grid of 1,084 values.  Every
parent has longitudinal half-width $7\times10^{-8}$ and transverse half-width
$10^{-8}$ in each of the five directions, with clipping only when interpreting
the union at the two declared path endpoints.  The smallest overlap width
between consecutive longitudinal parents is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:overlap}
 \frac{27}{18050000000}>0.
\end{equation}
For every parent the calculation stores two hypersurface and 81 D-term image
intervals.  Thus the full root layer contains $1084\times83=89972$ exact
componentwise strict inclusions.  Its smallest distance from an image endpoint
to the corresponding unknown-box boundary is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rootmargin}
 \frac{9335426793689657913651}
 {10910555522022028301723238400000}>0.
\end{equation}

\section{Gluing the local roots}

The Krawczyk theorem is local in the unknown variables.  A separate argument
is therefore needed before the 1,084 local maps may be called one branch.
The anchor is the previously certified robust continuation chain on the
central slice $w=0$.  It consists of seven Krawczyk patches and seven bridges,
covers $t\in[-3/20000,0]$, and has exact maximum image-to-radius ratios
\begin{equation}
 \frac{4808980629275713}{5661108081689003}<\frac{17}{20},\qquad
 \frac{5163879392018433}{6226644134068241}<\frac{17}{20}
\end{equation}
for patches and bridges, respectively.  All $7\times83$ patch and
$7\times83$ bridge component inequalities are replayed by the portable
verifier.

\begin{lemma}[Product branch gluing]\label{lem:glue}
Let $P_i$ be the ordered parent parameter boxes and $X_i$ their Krawczyk
uniqueness boxes.  Suppose: (i) each $P_i$ has a continuous locally unique
root map $x_i:P_i\to X_i$; (ii) at the center of the central slice $w=0$, the
robust continuation root is strictly nested in $X_i$; and (iii) every
$P_i\cap P_{i+1}$ is nonempty and connected.  Then the maps $x_i$ glue to one
continuous locally unique root branch on $\bigcup_iP_i$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On the connected central slice of $P_i$, consider the set where $x_i$ equals
the robust continuation root.  It is nonempty by the strict anchor nesting,
closed by continuity, and open by local uniqueness and strict interior
inclusion.  Hence the two maps agree throughout that slice.  On the connected
product overlap $P_i\cap P_{i+1}$, the analogous equality set for $x_i$ and
$x_{i+1}$ contains the nonempty central slice.  It is again open and closed,
so the two local maps agree on the overlap.  Induction over the 1,083 edges
gives the claimed branch.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Certified implicit branch]\label{prop:branch}
For every $(t,w)\in\TT$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:F} has a root on the branch selected by
the robust central anchor.  The root is locally unique in the union of the
declared Krawczyk boxes and depends continuously on $(t,w)$.
\end{proposition}

This proposition is deliberately local in $x$: it does not exclude unrelated
roots outside the certified boxes.

\section{Metric cover and positivity}

The root parent at a fixed longitudinal center serves 32 metric children.
For each transverse coordinate the two child intervals are
\begin{equation}
 [-10^{-8},0]\quad\text{and}\quad[0,10^{-8}],
\end{equation}
with centers $\mp5\times10^{-9}$ and half-width $5\times10^{-9}$.  Their
Cartesian products give exactly $2^5=32$ closed boxes.  Endpoint comparison
proves that their union is the parent transverse cube without a gap.  Across
all longitudinal centers there are
\begin{equation}
 1084\times32=34688
\end{equation}
metric boxes.

On every child, interval implicit differentiation supplies the six real first
and second derivatives required for the $3\times3$ Hermitian pullback tensor.
The inverse of $QDQ^T$ and the joint implicit Jacobian is enclosed using a
scaled finite Neumann series with a geometric remainder.  Second-order
interval jets analytically differentiate Eq.~\eqref{eq:correction}.  The
Hermitian tensor is finally intersected with its conjugate transpose.

Let $\Delta_k$ be the determinant of the leading $k\times k$ principal
submatrix of $\Gc$.  The Hermitian Sylvester criterion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sylvester}
 \Gc\succ0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Delta_1>0,\quad\Delta_2>0,\quad\Delta_3>0.
\end{equation}
All three directed intervals are stored on every child.  Table~\ref{tab:main}
lists the exact global minima of their lower endpoints.
The maximum ratio of a robust-anchor radius to its containing parent radius is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anchorratio}
 \frac{146013824139625356625}{244296187218972043640832}<1.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Corrected-metric positivity on the certified product tube]
\label{thm:main}
In the selected GLSM chart, for the exactly specified frozen ansatz of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:correction}, there is a locally unique parameterized root branch
on $\TT$.  Along that branch the corrected Hermitian pullback metric is
positive definite at every point of $\TT$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The exact parent grid covers the longitudinal interval, and the 32 child
templates tile each transverse cube.  The 1,084 parametric Krawczyk inclusions
give local root existence and uniqueness on all parents.  Lemma~\ref{lem:glue}
identifies these roots with one branch on their union.  On each of the 34,688
metric children, interval evaluation gives positive rational lower bounds for
all three leading principal minors.  Their global minima are the positive
fractions in Table~\ref{tab:main}.  Equation~\eqref{eq:sylvester} proves
positive definiteness on every child and therefore on all of $\TT$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Central-path corollary]
\label{prop:path}
On the central slice $u_1=\cdots=u_5=0$, the same locally unique branch
exists for $d\in[1.49985,1.5]$, and its corrected Hermitian pullback metric is
positive definite throughout the interval.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The slice is contained in $\TT$ under $t=d-1.5$.  The claim is therefore the
restriction of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  In particular, no sampling argument or
additional continuity assumption is used.
\end{proof}

Positivity alone does not show that the correction is quantitatively visible.

\begin{equation}
 \frac{7568816278373741}{413272176438128128}

This is an existence statement within the disclosed finite ensemble.  It is
not an operator-norm bound, a uniform statement along the whole path, or a
claim that the frozen correction is the complete physical $\alpha'$ series.
The independent endpoint calculation is not needed for
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}; its value is implementation diversity and a separate
exact consistency check at a shared point.

\section{Proof architecture and reproducibility}

The release has two deliberately different verification levels.

The \emph{quick verifier} uses only the Python standard library.  It checks
canonical reduced rational pairs, the exact integer registry, all robust-chain
inclusions, all 89,972 parent-root inclusions, strict derivative exclusion of
zero, robust-anchor nesting, exact longitudinal coverage, all 1,083 overlap
widths, the 32-box transverse tiling, and all 104,064 stored metric-minor
intervals.  It recomputes the minima in Table~\ref{tab:main}; serialized Boolean
verdicts and floating-point ratios are not accepted as proof inputs.  It also
reconstructs the independent endpoint determinants and the fixed-direction
materiality bounds from exact rational data.

The \emph{full regeneration} uses python-flint 0.9.0 with FLINT 3.6.0 and
100 decimal digits of Arb working precision \cite{Johansson2017}.  From the
frozen formula inputs it reconstructs every parent root enclosure and every
metric child enclosure, then compares all proof-relevant fields with the
archived release.  Diagnostic floating-point fields and runtime metadata are
excluded from this equality test.  The full calculation is resumable; its
checkpoint directory is explicitly outside the immutable proof manifest.
The release-level run completed all 1,084 root parents and all 34,688 metric
children; every proof-relevant regenerated field equals its archived value.

For a published release, the commands are
\begin{verbatim}
python verify_release.py --quick
python verify_release.py --full
\end{verbatim}
The first command is intended for routine reviewer replay.  The second is the
slower reconstruction audit.  Eleven mutation tests force equality in a Krawczyk
inclusion, put zero into a metric lower bound, delete a transverse child,
corrupt the integer geometry, break a robust anchor, and alter endpoint
evidence.  Each mutation must be rejected.  Finally, a SHA-256 manifest binds
every release file byte for byte.

This is a computer-assisted proof, not a proof in a foundational proof
assistant.  The quick layer trusts Python integer arithmetic, JSON parsing,
and SHA-256, as well as the mathematical implication from valid directed Arb
enclosures to their serialized rational supersets.  The regeneration layer
additionally trusts the disclosed formula implementation, Python, python-flint,
FLINT/Arb, the compiler, and hardware.  Separating the layers makes these
assumptions visible and prevents a cached Boolean from substituting for an
inequality.

\section{Scope and interpretation}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is stronger than a path-only theorem because $\TT$ has
nonzero extent in all five declared transverse directions.  It is nevertheless
local in several independent senses.

First, the theorem concerns one toric chart and one small product neighborhood.
It does not cover the whole compact hypersurface or establish compatibility
with other charts.  ``Positive on the $(5,81)$ Calabi--Yau'' without this
qualification would therefore be an overstatement.

Second, $\Gzero$ is a section-free GLSM quotient reference metric, not a
certified Ricci-flat Calabi--Yau metric.  Equation~\eqref{eq:correction} is one
frozen gauge-invariant approximation ansatz.  We prove positivity of that
specific tensor.  We do not bound a global Ricci or Monge--Amp\`ere residual,
derive a complete physical $\alpha'$ correction, or control omitted orders.

Third, the implicit theorem establishes local uniqueness in disclosed boxes.
It neither proves global uniqueness of the algebraic/moment-map system nor
excludes disconnected branches elsewhere in field space.

Finally, the geometry originates in a realistic compactification program, but
the present theorem says nothing by itself about the full 113-field system,
moduli stabilization, masses, uplift, metastability, or phenomenology.  It is
not a certificate of a physical vacuum.

\section{Conclusion}

We have consolidated a one-dimensional positivity calculation and its
independent endpoint audit into a uniform computer-assisted theorem on an
explicit six-real-dimensional product tube.  The former path theorem is now
the immediate central-slice corollary, while the endpoint calculation supplies
an implementation-diverse validation and a certified finite-ensemble witness
that the correction can exceed one percent.
The proof combines 1,084 parametric root parents, an anchored branch-gluing
argument, an exact $2^5$ transverse tiling, and 34,688 interval metric boxes.
Every final inequality is available for dependency-free rational replay, while
a separate Arb layer supports formula regeneration from frozen inputs.

The natural mathematical extensions are larger certified radii and overlapping
multi-chart coverage.  A physically stronger direction would require an
independent approximation theorem relating the frozen ansatz to a canonical
Ricci-flat or effective-action metric.  Neither extension is assumed in the
result reported here.
